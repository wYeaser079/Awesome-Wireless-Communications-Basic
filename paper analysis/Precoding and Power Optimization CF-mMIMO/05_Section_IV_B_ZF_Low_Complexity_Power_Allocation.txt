PART 1 — HIGH-LEVEL / LAYMAN'S EXPLANATION
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

In the previous file we saw that the optimal max-min power allocation for
ZF can be solved via bisection + a cheap linear-system check per step
(thanks to Lemma 1 and Corollary 1). That's already much better than the
CB case, but Section IV-B goes one step further: it gives a CLOSED-FORM
approximate power allocation that requires NO bisection at all, and
proves that this closed-form solution is NEAR-OPTIMAL when the uplink
pilot power is large enough.

THE BIG IDEA. Ask yourself: what does the max-min ZF problem look like if
we assume PERFECT channel estimation (no estimation error at all)? In
this "perfect CSI" regime, the SINR formula (13) simplifies enormously —
the denominator becomes just 1, because ν_{ki} = 0. The SINR is now
simply ρ_f · η_k, which is LINEAR in η_k. The max-min problem becomes a
LINEAR PROGRAM with per-AP constraints — trivially solvable.

LEMMA 2 gives the solution of this perfect-CSI max-min LP in CLOSED FORM:

  (i)  There exists at least one AP m₀ that transmits at full power.
  (ii) All K users receive the same power coefficient:
          η_1^pc = η_2^pc = ⋯ = η_K^pc = 1 / (Σᵢ ν_{m₀i}^pc).
  (iii) AP m₀ is the one with the LARGEST Σᵢ ν_{m₀i}^pc — the
        "bottleneck" AP.

In words: the optimal perfect-CSI solution is DETERMINED BY THE WORST AP.
You set all users' power to the maximum that AP m₀ can support, and
that's it.

NOW, THE AUTHORS' INSIGHT. Even though the REAL system has channel
estimation error, at high enough uplink pilot SNR the error is small
enough that the Lemma 2 formula is nearly optimal. They propose using
Eq. (21) directly:

  η_k^apx  =  1 / max_m (Σᵢ ν_mi),   same for all k,

where ν_mi is the REAL (not perfect-CSI) statistic defined in (15),
which INCLUDES the estimation error. No bisection, no iterative
solving, nothing. Just compute ν_mi, find the AP with the largest row
sum, and set η to its reciprocal.

PROPOSITION 2 makes this rigorous: for any ε > 0, there exists an uplink
pilot power threshold ρ_r(ε) such that, when the uplink power exceeds
this threshold, the SINR achieved by the closed-form η^apx differs from
the optimal SINR by at most ε. The proof uses continuity of the
optimal-solution map (Lemma 1) and the fact that ν → ν^pc as pilot
power grows.

COMPLEXITY. Computing η^apx costs essentially O(MK²), which is the same
as the cost of computing the ZF precoding matrix itself. So the power
allocation is "free" relative to the precoder computation. This is a
spectacular result: a near-optimal max-min power allocation for ZF at no
additional asymptotic complexity.

WHY THIS WORKS — INTUITIVELY.
  • Lemma 2 says the perfect-CSI solution is controlled by the bottleneck
    AP.
  • In reality, the bottleneck AP is usually the one closest to some
    user, because close APs have high γ values and thus high ν values.
  • Close APs have LOW channel estimation error (large γ / β ratio), so
    for THEM, perfect CSI is nearly true.
  • Far APs have high estimation error, but their ν_mi is small, so they
    don't affect the bottleneck.
  • Hence the formula that depends on the bottleneck AP's statistics is
    accurate in the regime that matters.

This elegant argument is one of the highlights of the paper: it transforms
a high-complexity iterative problem into a trivial formula, with rigorous
near-optimality guarantees.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1  THE PERFECT-CSI SIMPLIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Start from the ZF SINR formula (Eq. 13):

                           ρ_f · η_k
       SINR_{k,ZF}(η)  =  ──────────────────────
                           1 + ρ_f · Σᵢ η_i · ν_{ki}

where ν_{ki} is the interference leakage due to channel estimation error
(Eq. 14). Recall that ν_{ki} depends on the error variance (β_mk − γ_mk),
which vanishes as the uplink pilot SNR ρ_r · τ · β_mk → ∞.

SETTING ν_{ki} → 0 (perfect CSI):

       SINR_{k,ZF}^{pc}  =  ρ_f · η_k^{pc}.                        (19a)

The SINR is now LINEAR in the power coefficient. The max-min problem
becomes:

       maximize_{η^{pc}}   min_k  ρ_f · η_k^{pc}                   (19)

       subject to          Σᵢ₌₁ᴷ η_i^{pc} · ν_{mi}^{pc}  ≤  1,   ∀m, (19b)

where ν_{mi}^{pc} is the perfect-CSI version of the per-AP power
coefficient (Eq. 20):

       ν_m^{pc}  =  diag{ 𝔼[(GᵀG*)⁻¹ · g_{[m]} · g_{[m]}ᴴ · (GᵀG*)⁻¹] }
                                                                  (20)

obtained by replacing all Ĝ by G (true channel) in Eq. (15).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2  LEMMA 2 — CLOSED-FORM PERFECT-CSI SOLUTION (Appendix E)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STATEMENT. Let η^{pc} = (η_1^{pc}, …, η_K^{pc}) be the optimal solution of
(19). Then:

  (A) There exists at least one AP m₀ ∈ {1, …, M} that transmits at full
      power, i.e.,

             Σᵢ₌₁ᴷ η_i^{pc} · ν_{m₀ i}^{pc}  =  1.                  (full-power AP)

  (B) The optimal power coefficients are uniform and equal to:

             η_1^{pc}  =  η_2^{pc}  =  ⋯  =  η_K^{pc}
                        =  1 / (Σᵢ₌₁ᴷ ν_{m₀ i}^{pc}).               (uniform)

PROOF (reworked from Appendix E).

Step 1: Some AP must be at full power.

Suppose, for contradiction, that NO AP is at full power, i.e., for every
m, Σᵢ η_i^{pc} · ν_{mi}^{pc} < 1. Let p_m := Σᵢ η_i^{pc} · ν_{mi}^{pc}
denote the per-AP spent power fraction. Define

       p_max  :=  max_m  p_m  <  1.

Now SCALE UP the power coefficients: replace each η_i^{pc} by
η_i' := η_i^{pc} / p_max. Then η_i' > η_i^{pc} strictly. The new per-AP
usage becomes p_m / p_max, which is ≤ 1 for all m (with equality at the
m that achieved p_max). So η' is feasible, and its SINRs
ρ_f · η_i' > ρ_f · η_i^{pc}. This contradicts the optimality of η^{pc}. ∎
Step 1.

So there exists m₀ with Σᵢ η_i^{pc} · ν_{m₀ i}^{pc} = 1.

Step 2: All η_k^{pc} are equal.

Define:
       η_ℓ^{pc}  :=  min_k η_k^{pc}
       η_u^{pc}  :=  max_k η_k^{pc}.

Suppose (for contradiction) that η_ℓ^{pc} < η_u^{pc}. The paper's idea is
to CONSTRUCT a new feasible vector η̃^{pc} such that:

    - η̃_ℓ^{pc} > η_ℓ^{pc},
    - η̃_u^{pc} < η_u^{pc},
    - The minimum SINR IMPROVES: min_k SINR_k(η̃^{pc}) > min_k SINR_k(η^{pc}).

Under max-min, the min SINR equals ρ_f · η_ℓ (since η_ℓ is the smallest
component). If we can increase η_ℓ while satisfying per-AP constraints,
we improve the max-min SINR.

The key is to "steal" power from user u to give to user ℓ in a way that
respects the per-AP constraints. The paper does this by defining:

       c  :=  ν_{m₀ ℓ}^{pc} / ν_{m₀ u}^{pc}

(the ratio of the ℓ-th and u-th columns of ν_{m₀}, at the bottleneck AP).

If we transfer c units of η_u "backwards" into one unit of η_ℓ:
       η̃_ℓ  =  η_ℓ + δ,
       η̃_u  =  η_u − c · δ,
where δ > 0, the per-AP m₀ constraint change is:

       Δ(power at AP m₀)  =  ν_{m₀ ℓ}^{pc} · δ − ν_{m₀ u}^{pc} · (c · δ)
                           =  (ν_{m₀ ℓ}^{pc} − ν_{m₀ u}^{pc} · c) · δ
                           =  0.

So the transfer at AP m₀ is NEUTRAL. At OTHER APs m ≠ m₀, the change is:

       Δ(power at AP m)  =  (ν_{mℓ}^{pc} − ν_{mu}^{pc} · c) · δ.

This may be positive (violating feasibility) or negative (improving it).
To ensure feasibility, choose δ small enough that no AP's constraint is
violated. There always exists such a δ > 0 (because the constraints were
strict at m ≠ m₀).

With this transfer, η̃_ℓ = η_ℓ + δ > η_ℓ. The new minimum SINR is:
       min_k SINR_k(η̃)  =  ρ_f · min_k η̃_k.
Since we increased η_ℓ (previously the minimum) and the minimum is still
dominated by small values (which are now no smaller than η_ℓ + δ), the
min has strictly improved. This contradicts the optimality of η^{pc}. ∎
Step 2.

CONCLUSION. All η_k^{pc} are equal to some common value η^{pc}. From
the full-power AP m₀, we have:

       η^{pc} · (Σᵢ₌₁ᴷ ν_{m₀ i}^{pc})  =  1
       ⟹   η^{pc}  =  1 / (Σᵢ₌₁ᴷ ν_{m₀ i}^{pc}).

And since m₀ is the bottleneck (the AP with the largest Σᵢ ν_{mi}^{pc}),
we can state the final result as:

       η^{pc}  =  1 / ( max_m  Σᵢ₌₁ᴷ ν_{mi}^{pc} ).

This is Lemma 2. The solution is: uniform power, determined by the
worst-case AP. ∎

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3  THE HEURISTIC APPROXIMATE SOLUTION (Eq. 21)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Nayebi et al. take this beautiful perfect-CSI solution and USE IT
DIRECTLY with the real (non-perfect-CSI) statistics ν_{mi} from Eq. (15):

       η_1^apx  =  η_2^apx  =  ⋯  =  η_K^apx
               =  1 / ( max_m  Σᵢ₌₁ᴷ ν_{mi} ).                     (21)

Note the CRUCIAL difference from Lemma 2: we use ν_{mi} (which INCLUDES
channel estimation error effects) in the real system, not ν_{mi}^{pc}.
The closed-form structure (uniform η, reciprocal of bottleneck row sum)
is borrowed from the perfect-CSI case, but the ν values are real.

The resulting SINR (plugging η^apx into Eq. 13):

       SINR_{k,ZF}(η^apx)  =  (ρ_f · η^apx) / (1 + ρ_f · η^apx · Σᵢ ν_{ki})
                            =  1 / (1/(ρ_f · η^apx) + Σᵢ ν_{ki}).

This is generally DIFFERENT for different k, because the ν_{ki} row sums
differ across k. So the heuristic does NOT equalize SINRs (the true
max-min would). But it is a cheap approximation, and Proposition 2
proves it is near-optimal for large uplink pilot power.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4  WHY IS η^apx NEAR-OPTIMAL? — INTUITIVE ARGUMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The paper gives an intuitive sketch. Recall from Section II (Eq. 2) that

       Var(~g_mk) / Var(ĝ_mk)  =  (β_mk − γ_mk) / γ_mk
                                 =  1 / (ρ_r · τ · β_mk).

So the ratio of estimation-error power to estimate power at (AP m, user k)
is inversely proportional to ρ_r · τ · β_mk.

For APs CLOSE TO USER k (large β_mk), this ratio is SMALL — estimation is
nearly perfect. For APs FAR FROM USER k (small β_mk), the ratio is large
— estimation is poor.

Now, the ZF precoder's contribution to user k's signal is dominated by
the close APs (they have large β_mk, hence large contributions through
the pseudoinverse). Far APs contribute little to the precoded signal, so
even if their channel estimates are noisy, the NOISE IN THE CONTRIBUTION
is small in absolute terms.

In other words: where estimation is accurate, the signal is strong; where
estimation is inaccurate, the signal is weak anyway. The error and the
useful signal are BOTH CONCENTRATED NEAR THE USER, so perfect-CSI analysis
is approximately correct.

This is why the perfect-CSI Lemma 2 formula carries over so well to the
real-world case.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.5  PROPOSITION 2 — RIGOROUS ε-OPTIMALITY (Appendix F)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STATEMENT. Let η^opt and η^apx respectively denote the optimal solution
of the max-min ZF problem (17) and the power coefficients defined by
(21). For every ε > 0, there exists a threshold ρ_r(ε) such that whenever
the uplink pilot transmit power ρ_r > ρ_r(ε),

       | SINR_{k,ZF}(η^opt) − SINR_{k,ZF}(η^apx) |  <  ε,   ∀k.

In words: with enough uplink pilot power, the heuristic is arbitrarily
close to optimal.

PROOF (sketched from Appendix F).

The proof uses THREE continuity arguments.

(1) CONTINUITY OF η^opt IN (ν_k, ν_m).
    From Lemma 1 we know that the optimal power vector is the unique
    solution of a linear system (I − tB)η = d. Since the entries of B
    and d depend continuously on ν_k and ν_m, and since matrix inversion
    is continuous away from singularities, η^opt(ν_k, ν_m) is a
    continuous function. Formally: for every ε₁ > 0 there exist
    ε_k, ε_m > 0 such that
       ‖η^opt(ν_k, ν_m) − η^opt(ν_k^pc, ν_m^pc)‖ < ε₁/2
    whenever ‖ν_k − ν_k^pc‖ < ε_k and ‖ν_m − ν_m^pc‖ < ε_m.

(2) CONTINUITY OF η^apx IN ν_m.
    The formula (21) involves a max over m of a linear function of ν_m,
    followed by a reciprocal. Both are continuous. So:
       ‖η^apx(ν_m) − η^apx(ν_m^pc)‖ < ε₁/2 whenever ‖ν_m − ν_m^pc‖ < ε₂.

(3) CONVERGENCE ν → ν^pc AS ρ_r → ∞.
    Recall γ_mk = ρ_r τ β_mk² / (1 + ρ_r τ β_mk) → β_mk as ρ_r → ∞.
    Consequently ĝ_mk → g_mk (in the L² sense) and the matrices
    (ĜᵀĜ*)⁻¹ → (GᵀG*)⁻¹ (in probability, by continuous mapping).
    The expectations ν_m, ν_k converge to ν_m^pc, ν_k^pc as ρ_r → ∞.
    So for any ε_k, ε_m > 0, there exists ρ_r(ε_k, ε_m) such that
       ‖ν_k − ν_k^pc‖ < ε_k  and  ‖ν_m − ν_m^pc‖ < ε_m
    for all ρ_r > ρ_r(ε_k, ε_m).

(4) COMBINE. By Lemma 2, η^opt(ν_k^pc, ν_m^pc) = η^apx(ν_m^pc), i.e.,
    the perfect-CSI optimal matches the heuristic (21) EXACTLY at
    perfect CSI. So:
       ‖η^opt(ν_k, ν_m) − η^apx(ν_m)‖
       ≤  ‖η^opt(ν_k, ν_m) − η^opt(ν_k^pc, ν_m^pc)‖
        + ‖η^apx(ν_m^pc) − η^apx(ν_m)‖                 (triangle)
       <  ε₁/2 + ε₁/2 = ε₁.

    Finally, SINR_{k,ZF}(η) is a continuous function of η, so small
    deviation in η yields small deviation in SINR: for any ε > 0 there
    exists ε₁ > 0 such that
       ‖η^opt − η^apx‖ < ε₁
    ⟹  |SINR_{k,ZF}(η^opt) − SINR_{k,ZF}(η^apx)| < ε.

    Putting it all together, for any ε > 0 there exists ρ_r(ε) such
    that whenever ρ_r > ρ_r(ε), the heuristic is ε-optimal. ∎

REMARK. The paper notes that since all norms on a finite-dimensional
Banach space are equivalent, the choice of norm (1-norm, 2-norm, ∞-norm,
Frobenius norm) does not matter for the statement.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.6  COMPLEXITY OF COMPUTING η^apx
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The paper's cost analysis is worth reading carefully. The dominant
step in computing η^apx via Eq. (21) is computing the M vectors ν_m
(each of length K) from Eq. (15):

       ν_{m,curr}  =  diag{ (ĜᵀĜ*)⁻¹ · ĝ_[m] · ĝ_[m]ᴴ · (ĜᵀĜ*)⁻¹ }.

Let's count operations for ONE AP m:
  (a) Compute ĜᵀĜ*: cost O(M K²) for the whole matrix (done once for
      all m). The multiplication of K×M and M×K matrices dominates.
  (b) Invert (ĜᵀĜ*): cost O(K³). Also done once.
  (c) For each m, compute (ĜᵀĜ*)⁻¹ · ĝ_[m]: matrix-vector product,
      cost O(K²) per m, total O(M K²) for all m.
  (d) For each m, compute ĝ_[m]ᴴ · (above result) giving a scalar;
      also O(K) per m.
  (e) For each m, compute outer product ĝ_[m] · ĝ_[m]ᴴ and contract
      with (ĜᵀĜ*)⁻¹ twice; O(K²) per m.
  (f) Total for all m: O(M K²).

UNDER THE ASSUMPTION M ≫ K:
  Dominant cost  =  O(M K²).

This is EXACTLY THE SAME as the cost of computing the ZF precoding
matrix A = Ĝ* (ĜᵀĜ*)⁻¹. So the power allocation is FREE relative to
the precoder computation. If the NC was going to compute the precoder
anyway (which it must, to actually transmit), computing η^apx adds no
asymptotic burden.

CONTRAST WITH OPTIMAL (17) via Lemma 1 / Corollary 1.
  Per bisection step:  O(K³) (linear system solve).
  Number of bisection steps:  O(log(1/ε)).
  Plus ν_m computation: O(M K²).
  Total:  O(M K² + K³ · log(1/ε)).

For M = 128, K = 16, ε = 10⁻⁴:
  MK² = 128 · 256 = 32,768.
  K³ · log(1/ε) = 4096 · 14 = 57,344.
  Sum ≈ 90,000 operations.

For η^apx:
  MK² = 32,768.

The savings are about 2.7× in this regime. For larger K (say K = 64), the
gap grows because K³ scales cubically:
  For M = 512, K = 64:
    MK² = 2,097,152.
    K³ · log(1/ε) = 262,144 · 14 ≈ 3,670,016.
  η^apx: 2,097,152.
  Optimal: 5,767,168.
  Savings: 2.75× — same ratio.

So η^apx is consistently faster by a factor of ~2-3 for typical
parameters. More importantly, η^apx requires NO bisection outer loop,
making it much simpler to implement.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.7  PRACTICAL SIGNIFICANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The result is striking: a closed-form heuristic, borrowed from a toy
perfect-CSI model, is provably near-optimal for the real-world problem.

Three practical consequences:

  1. IMPLEMENTATION IS TRIVIAL. No iterative solvers, no SOCPs, no
     convex programming libraries. Just matrix arithmetic.

  2. REAL-TIME OPERATION. Power updates can be computed in a single
     fronthaul roundtrip, with no wait for convergence.

  3. PREDICTABLE LATENCY. The cost is exactly O(MK²) per update — no
     variance due to SOCP iteration counts or bisection depth.

This is the kind of result that turns an academic algorithm into
deployable infrastructure.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  CLOSED-FORM POWER ALLOCATIONS IN MASSIVE MIMO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Closed-form power allocation formulas are a recurring theme in massive
MIMO because the distributed / large-dimensional structure often
produces "bottleneck" solutions. Key references:

  • H. Q. Ngo, E. G. Larsson, T. L. Marzetta, "The multicell multiuser
    MIMO uplink with very large antenna arrays and a finite-dimensional
    channel," IEEE Trans. Commun., vol. 61, no. 6, pp. 2350–2361,
    Jun. 2013. [Asymptotic performance analysis via large-system limits.]

  • E. Björnson, M. Kountouris, M. Debbah, "Massive MIMO and small cells:
    Improving energy efficiency by optimal soft-cell coordination,"
    Proc. 20th ICT, 2013. [Per-AP power constraints in heterogeneous
    networks.]

  • T. Van Chien, E. Björnson, E. G. Larsson, "Joint power allocation
    and user association optimization for massive MIMO systems," IEEE
    Trans. Wireless Commun., vol. 15, no. 9, pp. 6384–6399, Sep. 2016.
    [Max-min + joint association, similar fractional linear structure.]

The Nayebi et al. 2017 contribution is one of the first closed-form
near-optimal results for CELL-FREE (rather than cellular) massive MIMO.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2  CONTINUITY ARGUMENTS FOR PERTURBED OPTIMIZATION PROBLEMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The proof of Proposition 2 uses a "perturbation-of-optimizer" argument
that has a long history in optimization theory, going back to:

  • A. V. Fiacco, "Sensitivity analysis for nonlinear programming using
    penalty methods," Math. Programming, vol. 10, no. 1, pp. 287–311, 1976.

  • J. F. Bonnans, A. Shapiro, Perturbation Analysis of Optimization
    Problems, Springer, 2000. [Comprehensive treatment.]

The key facts used:
  (a) The optimal value of a parametric optimization problem is
      continuous in the parameter (under mild regularity conditions).
  (b) The optimizer itself is continuous when it is unique.

In the paper's context, the "parameter" is the matrix pair (ν_k, ν_m),
and the "optimization problem" is (17). Lemma 1 establishes uniqueness
of the optimal solution (up to equality), which is why continuity applies.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3  PILOT POWER SCALING IN MASSIVE MIMO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The argument that "for large ρ_r, estimation is good and perfect CSI
analysis applies" is a staple of Massive MIMO asymptotics:

  • H. Q. Ngo, E. G. Larsson, T. L. Marzetta, "Energy and spectral
    efficiency of very large multiuser MIMO systems," IEEE Trans.
    Commun., vol. 61, no. 4, pp. 1436–1449, Apr. 2013.

They show that when the uplink pilot power ρ_r is scaled as (constant)/√M
or similar, the massive MIMO array gain still dominates and perfect CSI
performance is asymptotically achieved. The Nayebi et al. Proposition 2
is a finite-dimensional, non-asymptotic version of the same intuition,
stated as an ε-threshold bound.

│     |SINR_{k,ZF}(η^opt) − SINR_{k,ZF}(η^apx)|  <  ε,  k=1,…,K.          │
│                                                                          │
│   Proof: Appendix F, using continuity of η^opt (Lemma 1),                │
│   continuity of η^apx (Eq. 21), and ν → ν^pc as ρ_r → ∞.                │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ COMPLEXITY:                                                              │
│                                                                          │
│   Computing η^apx (Eq. 21):      O(M K²)                                │
│   Computing η^opt (via bisection + Lemma 1/Corollary 1):                │
│                                    O(M K² + K³ · log(1/ε))             │
│   ZF precoding matrix A:          O(M K²)                                │
│                                                                          │
│   ⟹ η^apx is "free" relative to the ZF precoder computation.            │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

PART 5 — NUMERICAL EXAMPLE
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1  EXAMPLE 1: APPLYING EQ. (21) TO A SMALL SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Take the same M = 3, K = 2, perfect-CSI example from Section IV Part 1
File, Section 5.1. We had:

   ν_{mi} matrix:
       ν = |  0.160    0.0292 |   AP 1
           |  0.040    0.00325|   AP 2
           |  0.040    0.264  |   AP 3

Row sums:
   AP 1: 0.160 + 0.0292 = 0.189
   AP 2: 0.040 + 0.00325 = 0.04325
   AP 3: 0.040 + 0.264 = 0.304

max_m (row sum) = 0.304, at AP 3.

η^apx = 1 / 0.304 ≈ 3.29.

By Lemma 2, this is the exact perfect-CSI optimal. With real ν (including
estimation error), it's an approximation; Proposition 2 says it's within
ε of optimum for high uplink pilot power.

PER-USER SINR (assuming ρ_f = 1 and ν_{ki} ≈ 0 for illustration):
   SINR_1 = SINR_2 = ρ_f · η^apx = 3.29.
   R = log₂(4.29) ≈ 2.10 bits/s/Hz.

Compare with CB optimal max-min (from file 2 of Section III) — which
required bisection + SOCP — giving a similar rate but at much higher
computational cost.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2  EXAMPLE 2: WITH NON-ZERO ν_{ki}
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Suppose now there is non-zero channel estimation error, and for the SAME
example we have (roughly):

   ν_{ki} matrix (2×2, user-by-user):
       ν_k = |  0.05    0.02  |
             |  0.02    0.07  |

Using η^apx = 3.29 for both users:

   SINR_1  =  ρ_f · η^apx / (1 + ρ_f · (η^apx · 0.05 + η^apx · 0.02))
          =  3.29 / (1 + 3.29 · 0.07)
          =  3.29 / (1 + 0.230)
          =  3.29 / 1.230
          ≈  2.67.
   R_1  =  log₂(3.67)  ≈  1.88  bits/s/Hz.

   SINR_2  =  3.29 / (1 + 3.29 · (0.02 + 0.07))
          =  3.29 / (1 + 0.296)
          =  3.29 / 1.296
          ≈  2.54.
   R_2  =  log₂(3.54)  ≈  1.82  bits/s/Hz.

Minimum rate ≈ 1.82 bits/s/Hz.

The TRUE max-min optimal (17) would re-balance η to equalize SINR_1 and
SINR_2. In this case, user 2 has slightly higher interference (ν_{2,1}+
ν_{2,2} = 0.09 vs. ν_{1,1}+ν_{1,2} = 0.07), so the optimal would spend
slightly MORE on user 2 and slightly LESS on user 1 — but the
improvement is small (a fraction of a bit/s/Hz).

Proposition 2's guarantee: the gap shrinks to zero as ρ_r → ∞.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.3  PRACTICE PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM. Consider a cell-free system with M = 5 APs and K = 4 users. The
(real) ν_{mi} matrix (5×4) is:

       ν = |  0.10   0.05   0.02   0.03 |   AP 1
           |  0.08   0.06   0.04   0.02 |   AP 2
           |  0.05   0.12   0.02   0.04 |   AP 3
           |  0.03   0.02   0.15   0.04 |   AP 4
           |  0.02   0.03   0.04   0.18 |   AP 5

The interference leakage matrix ν_{ki} (user-by-user, 4×4) is:

       ν_k = |  0.08   0.03   0.01   0.02 |   user 1
             |  0.03   0.10   0.02   0.01 |   user 2
             |  0.01   0.02   0.09   0.03 |   user 3
             |  0.02   0.01   0.03   0.07 |   user 4

ρ_f = 1 (normalized).

(a) Compute η^apx using Eq. (21).
(b) Compute SINR_k for each user k under η^apx.
(c) Compute R_k = log₂(1 + SINR_k) for each user.
(d) What is the minimum rate?

SOLUTION.

(a) Row sums of ν:
      AP 1:  0.10 + 0.05 + 0.02 + 0.03 = 0.20
      AP 2:  0.08 + 0.06 + 0.04 + 0.02 = 0.20
      AP 3:  0.05 + 0.12 + 0.02 + 0.04 = 0.23
      AP 4:  0.03 + 0.02 + 0.15 + 0.04 = 0.24
      AP 5:  0.02 + 0.03 + 0.04 + 0.18 = 0.27
    max_m (row sum) = 0.27, at AP 5.
    η^apx = 1 / 0.27 ≈ 3.704.
    (All four users get the same coefficient.)

(b) For each user k, compute Σᵢ η^apx · ν_{ki} = η^apx · Σᵢ ν_{ki}:
      User 1: 3.704 · (0.08+0.03+0.01+0.02) = 3.704 · 0.14 = 0.519.
      User 2: 3.704 · (0.03+0.10+0.02+0.01) = 3.704 · 0.16 = 0.593.
      User 3: 3.704 · (0.01+0.02+0.09+0.03) = 3.704 · 0.15 = 0.556.
      User 4: 3.704 · (0.02+0.01+0.03+0.07) = 3.704 · 0.13 = 0.481.

    SINR_k = ρ_f · η^apx / (1 + ρ_f · η^apx · Σᵢ ν_{ki})
          = 3.704 / (1 + above row sum)
    User 1: 3.704 / 1.519 ≈ 2.438.
    User 2: 3.704 / 1.593 ≈ 2.325.
    User 3: 3.704 / 1.556 ≈ 2.381.
    User 4: 3.704 / 1.481 ≈ 2.501.

(c) Rates:
    R_1 = log₂(1 + 2.438) = log₂(3.438) ≈ 1.782 bits/s/Hz.
    R_2 = log₂(1 + 2.325) = log₂(3.325) ≈ 1.734 bits/s/Hz.
    R_3 = log₂(1 + 2.381) = log₂(3.381) ≈ 1.757 bits/s/Hz.
    R_4 = log₂(1 + 2.501) = log₂(3.501) ≈ 1.808 bits/s/Hz.

(d) Minimum rate = R_2 ≈ 1.734 bits/s/Hz (user 2 has the worst
    interference statistics).

OBSERVATION. The heuristic gives SINRs that vary slightly across users
(user 4 has the lowest interference and thus the highest SINR). The
true max-min optimum would equalize these — increasing η for user 2
slightly and decreasing it for user 4. The improvement in min rate
would be small (~0.02-0.05 bits/s/Hz), at the cost of running a full
bisection + linear system solver. For most engineering purposes, the
heuristic is quite sufficient.

WHAT WE COVERED IN THIS FILE:

  • Perfect-CSI version of the ZF max-min problem (Eq. 19-20).
  • LEMMA 2: solution is uniform power η^{pc}, bottleneck AP
    transmits at full power, explicit formula η^{pc} = 1 / max_m Σᵢ ν^{pc}_{mi}.
  • Proof of Lemma 2 via "power transfer" argument (Appendix E).
  • THE CLOSED-FORM HEURISTIC (Eq. 21): borrow the Lemma 2 formula with
    real ν_{mi} values.
  • Intuitive argument for near-optimality (small estimation error near
    the bottleneck AP).
  • PROPOSITION 2: rigorous ε-optimality for large uplink pilot power,
    proved via continuity of η^opt, η^apx, and ν → ν^{pc} (Appendix F).
  • Complexity analysis: η^apx costs O(MK²), same as ZF precoder —
    "free" relative to the precoder itself.

KEY TAKEAWAYS:

  1. The perfect-CSI optimal power allocation has a remarkable CLOSED
     FORM: uniform power, bottleneck AP at full power.
  2. Using this closed form directly with REAL ν values gives a
     near-optimal heuristic — SIMPLICITY HIDDEN IN COMPLEXITY.
  3. Proposition 2 makes the "near-optimal" statement rigorous with
     an ε-threshold bound.
  4. The heuristic is as cheap as computing the ZF precoder itself,
     making it asymptotically free.
  5. This is one of the paper's most elegant results: a
     PERFECT-CSI TOY PROBLEM yields an ALGORITHM that works in the
     REAL IMPERFECT-CSI WORLD.
