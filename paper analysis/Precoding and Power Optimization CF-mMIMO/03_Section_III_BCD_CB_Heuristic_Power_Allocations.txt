PART 1 — HIGH-LEVEL / LAYMAN'S EXPLANATION
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

In the previous file, we saw that the OPTIMAL max-min power allocation for
Conjugate Beamforming (Eq. 6) can be solved by bisection + SOCP — but the
cost scales badly with M and K. For real systems with M = 128 APs and
K = 16 users, that's a lot of variables to wrestle with every few seconds.

Sections III-B, III-C, III-D replace the optimal solution with three
progressively CHEAPER approximations:

  III-B  "FULL POWER TRANSMISSION" (Eq. 8):
         The naïve idea — every AP spends its entire power budget.
         The problem becomes CONVEX (not just quasi-concave), so it is
         easier to solve, but numerically gives TERRIBLE 5%-outage. The
         authors use this as a BASELINE to demonstrate that full-power
         is wrong, and to prove the counterintuitive claim "most APs
         should transmit at less than full power".

  III-C  "HEURISTIC FIXED-POWER" (Eq. 9):
         An offline statistical study of the optimal solution reveals
         that at the max-min optimum, the per-AP power p_m* correlates
         strongly with β_m^max = max_k β_mk. The pattern is roughly
         EXPONENTIAL DECAY:
                p_m = e^(−α · β_m^max).
         APs CLOSE to a user (large β_m^max) reduce their power; APs
         far from any user (small β_m^max) transmit at full power.
         The paper fits the exponent α once, offline, and then uses the
         rule online. The saved online computation is one convex problem
         (instead of a whole bisection cascade of them). Performance
         recovers ~140% of the gain lost by full power.

  III-D  "HEURISTIC UNIFORM COEFFICIENTS" (Eq. 10):
         The EXTREME simplification. Every AP m uses the same
         exponential power rule as in III-C, AND distributes its
         total power EQUALLY across all K users (η_mk = η_m
         for all k). Under this assumption, the per-AP constraint
         Σᵢ η_m · γ_mi = p_m directly gives a closed-form formula for
         η_m. No optimization at all. Each AP can compute its own η_m
         without talking to the NC — i.e., the whole power-allocation
         problem becomes DECENTRALIZED. This is a HUGE win for
         backhaul: the NC only needs to send the pre-fitted exponent α.

The stunning thing is that III-D, the simplest possible scheme, gives
performance nearly as good as III-C (which is near-optimal), when M ≫ K.

The conceptual message is deeper than algorithmic: it tells us that in a
massive distributed antenna system, LOCAL power decisions based on
long-term statistics work almost as well as global optimization. This is
the bedrock that later "scalable" cell-free papers (Björnson-Sanguinetti
2020) will build on.

2.1  SUB-SECTION III-B: "FULL POWER TRANSMISSION"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The premise: force every AP to spend its entire power budget. Equation (6b)
becomes an equality:

       Σᵢ₌₁ᴷ η_mi · γ_mi  =  1,    ∀m.                              (8b)

This lets us SIMPLIFY the SINR denominator. Start from the interference
expression derived in Appendix A:

       1  +  ρ_f · Σᵢ Σₘ η_mi · β_mk · γ_mi.

Swap the summation order:
       =  1  +  ρ_f · Σₘ β_mk · (Σᵢ η_mi γ_mi)
       =  1  +  ρ_f · Σₘ β_mk · 1                                  (by (8b))
       =  1  +  ρ_f · Σₘ β_mk.

This is a CONSTANT w.r.t. η. So the SINR denominator becomes a pure
function of β (not η), and ONLY the numerator involves η.

The problem (8) then reads:

       max_η   min_k  SINR_k(η)
              =  max_η   min_k   [ ρ_f · (Σₘ √(η_mk) γ_mk)² ] /
                                  [ 1 + ρ_f · Σₘ β_mk ]             (8a)

              s.t.       Σᵢ η_mi γ_mi = 1,  ∀m.                     (8b)

Because the denominator is a constant, maximizing the ratio is equivalent
to maximizing the numerator. And since log and squaring are monotonic,
it's also equivalent to maximizing

       min_k  Σₘ √(η_mk) · γ_mk
              ─────────────────
              √(1 + ρ_f Σₘ β_mk)

which is a min over k of LINEAR functions in the variables u_mk = √(η_mk).
Actually not quite — the constraint Σᵢ η_mi γ_mi = 1 is quadratic in
u_mi: Σᵢ u²_mi γ_mi = 1. So this is a linear objective over a quadratic
equality — a SPHERE intersected with the non-negative orthant (one sphere
per AP), plus K linear lower bounds.

PROPOSITION 1 (paper). The objective function min_k SINR_k(η) in (8a) is
a concave function of η, and the problem (8) is convex.

PROOF SKETCH (from Appendix B, reworked cleanly).

The denominator (1 + ρ_f Σₘ β_mk) is a constant w.r.t. η, so the ratio is
proportional to the numerator (Σₘ √η_mk γ_mk)². But a squared sum of
square roots is NOT concave in η in general — we need to show it.

Let's define:
       c_mk  :=  γ_mk / √( 1/ρ_f + Σₘ₌₁ᴹ β_mk ).

Then (with the constant denominator absorbed) we have:

       SINR_k(η)  =  ( Σₘ c_mk · √(η_mk) )².

The paper shows that (Σₘ c_mk · √η_mk)² is a concave function of η on the
non-negative orthant by a DIRECT AM-GM argument. Given η, η' ≥ 0 and
θ ∈ [0, 1]:

  SINR_k(θη + (1−θ)η')
  = ( Σₘ c_mk · √( θη_mk + (1−θ)η'_mk ) )².

Square and expand; the cross term has √((θη_mk+(1−θ)η'_mk)(θη_nk+(1−θ)η'_nk))
  which equals √(θ²·η_mk η_nk + (1−θ)²·η'_mk η'_nk + θ(1−θ)·[η_mk η'_nk + η_nk η'_mk]).

Apply the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality:

       η_mk η'_nk + η_nk η'_mk  ≥  2 · √(η_mk η'_nk η_nk η'_mk)
                                  =  2 · √(η_mk η_nk) · √(η'_mk η'_nk).

Substituting this lower bound back gives:

       √(θ²·η_mk η_nk + (1−θ)²·η'_mk η'_nk + 2θ(1−θ)·√(η_mk η_nk)·√(η'_mk η'_nk))
     = | θ·√(η_mk η_nk)  +  (1−θ)·√(η'_mk η'_nk) |
     = θ·√(η_mk η_nk)  +  (1−θ)·√(η'_mk η'_nk).

Summing over m,n, the squared form on the LHS becomes ≥ θ·SINR_k(η) +
(1−θ)·SINR_k(η'), which is exactly the DEFINITION OF CONCAVITY for
SINR_k(η) in the variable η (not the square-root variable!).

Since each SINR_k is concave in η and the minimum of concave functions is
concave, the objective of (8) is concave. The constraints (8b) are linear
in η. Therefore (8) is a CONVEX problem.  ∎

WHY THIS MATTERS. A convex problem with a concave objective and linear
constraints can be solved in ONE SHOT with any standard interior-point
method — no bisection needed. Complexity is drastically lower than (6).

But the price is that full-power transmission IS NOT OPTIMAL — the
paper's Figure 5 shows that full-power transmission loses more than
100% of the 5%-outage rate relative to the true optimum of (6).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2  WHY IS FULL POWER NOT OPTIMAL?  (The counterintuitive observation)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Intuitively: if you're trying to maximize throughput, why would you
transmit at less than full power? Doesn't more power always help?

The answer lies in the STRUCTURE OF THE SINR. The problem (6) is
QUADRATIC (the numerator is (Σₘ √η_mk γ_mk)²). Unlike LINEAR
optimization, where the optimum always lies at a vertex of the feasible
set (i.e., at full power), QUADRATIC optimization can have its optimum
in the INTERIOR of the feasible set.

The paper gives the exact mechanism via Figure 3 (scatter plot of
p_m^opt / ρ_f vs β_m^max over many channel realizations). The pattern
is:

  • For M = 8 APs: most APs transmit at full power. Not much structure.
  • For M = 32: a visible trend where APs with large β_m^max transmit
    at LESS than full power.
  • For M = 128: the trend is strong and roughly exponential:
           p_m^opt / ρ_f  ≈  e^(−α · β_m^max),
    where α > 0 depends on M and K.

INTUITION FOR THIS RULE:
  - β_m^max = max_k β_mk is large when AP m has at least one NEARBY user.
  - A nearby AP creates STRONG signal to its own user but also STRONG
    interference to OTHER users within the same "cell footprint".
  - In a max-min problem, if the nearby AP shouts at full power, it
    forces OTHER users' SINRs down (via β_mk · γ_mi cross-products), and
    the minimum over users drops.
  - The optimal compromise: the nearby AP should REDUCE its power so it
    helps its "owner" user just enough without blasting interference into
    other users.
  - APs FAR from all users (small β_m^max) have negligible interference
    impact and should transmit at full power — every bit of signal they
    contribute is useful without creating harm elsewhere.

This is an INSTANCE of the classical "interference-limited" regime: when
the system is limited by multi-user interference (not noise), the optimal
strategy is not "maximum power" but "balanced power". It is reminiscent of
the WATER-FILLING solution in information theory, where power is poured
only into non-full channels.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3  SUB-SECTION III-C: "HEURISTIC FIXED-POWER TRANSMISSION"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The authors formalize the observation into an algorithm.

STEP 1 (offline, one time).
  (a) Pick a representative (M, K) pair for the system.
  (b) Solve the optimal power allocation (6) for 100+ random channel
      realizations.
  (c) Collect all pairs ( β_m^max, p_m^opt / ρ_f ) — Figure 3 in the paper.
  (d) Fit a function p_m^fit(β_m^max) = e^(−α · β_m^max) to the scatter.
      The exponent α is chosen (e.g., via least-squares regression on
      log-transformed data) to best match the cloud.

This offline step is expensive (many bisections × many channel
realizations × curve fitting), but it is amortized over the entire
lifetime of the deployment. It does not contribute to online complexity.

STEP 2 (online, every time β_mk changes).
  Define
       p_m  :=  e^(−α · β_m^max),   m = 1, …, M.        (heuristic fit)

  Replace the "≤ 1" constraint in (6b) by an EQUALITY "= p_m":

       Σᵢ η_mi · γ_mi  =  p_m,    ∀m.                                (9b)

  The max-min problem becomes:

       max_η    min_k   ρ_f · (Σₘ √(η_mk) γ_mk)² /
                         (1 + ρ_f · Σₘ p_m · β_mk)                   (9a)

       s.t.     Σᵢ η_mi γ_mi = p_m,    ∀m.                           (9b)

Now the denominator of SINR_k is again CONSTANT in η (just as in III-B,
because each Σᵢ η_mi γ_mi is fixed at p_m). So (9) is EXACTLY the same
structure as (8) — a concave-objective problem with linear equality
constraints — and hence CONVEX. Proposition 1's proof applies verbatim.

COMPARISON TO FULL POWER (III-B):
  • Same online complexity: one convex problem.
  • But with RIGHT per-AP power budgets rather than the uniform p_m = 1.
  • Numerical result: ~140% improvement in 5%-outage rate vs. full power
    (paper, Figure 5).

WHY IS (9) BETTER THAN (8)? Because in (8), every AP is forced to spend
its entire budget — including APs close to users, which hurts the
max-min. In (9), APs close to users spend LESS (via e^(−α β_m^max)),
reducing the β_mk · γ_mi interference terms, and boosting the
minimum SINR.

The catch is that the exponential fit is HEURISTIC. For a different
(M, K) than the one fitted, the rule may become less accurate. But the
paper's Remark 2 notes that the exponential behavior only holds for
large M anyway — exactly the Massive MIMO regime where the paper lives.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4  SUB-SECTION III-D: "HEURISTIC UNIFORM POWER COEFFICIENTS"
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Now the authors push the simplification to the limit.

ASSUMPTION 1 (from III-C). AP m transmits with total power
       p_m  =  e^(−α · β_m^max).

ASSUMPTION 2 (new, for III-D). At AP m, the power coefficients η_mk are
equal for all users:
       η_mk  =  η_m,   ∀k.                                        (D.1)

In other words, the AP does NOT differentiate between users when deciding
how much power to spend. It just uses one scalar η_m.

Plugging (D.1) into the per-AP budget:

       Σᵢ₌₁ᴷ η_m · γ_mi  =  p_m
       ⟹   η_m  =  p_m  /  Σᵢ γ_mi.                               (10)

This is a CLOSED-FORM FORMULA. No optimization whatsoever. Each AP can
compute its own η_m from:
  (i)  β_m^max (its own strongest β, available at AP m),
  (ii) Σᵢ γ_mi (AP m can compute γ_mi from its own β_mi, ρ_r, τ),
  (iii) the global constant α (sent by the NC once, offline).

RESULT: the power allocation is FULLY DECENTRALIZED. Each AP acts
independently. The NC is not needed for power updates. This is HUGE:
  • Zero online computation at the NC for power updates.
  • Zero backhaul traffic for conveying η_mk from NC to APs.
  • Trivial to scale to arbitrary M.

WHAT DOES THIS COST IN PERFORMANCE?
  • Relative to the optimal (6): a small but measurable loss.
  • Relative to the heuristic fixed-power (9): the loss is small when
    M ≫ K (the Massive MIMO regime).
  • The paper quotes that (10) is "quite close" to the performance of (9)
    and much better than full power (8).

The INTUITIVE REASON why uniform coefficients are nearly optimal in the
M ≫ K regime:

     When M is large, each user has MANY APs contributing, and the per-AP
     coefficient η_mk matters less than the TOTAL power p_m spent at
     AP m. The "mix" of power across users at each AP gets averaged out
     by the large number of serving APs. Hence, how AP m divides its
     budget among users is less important than how large the budget is.

This is a flavor of the law-of-large-numbers argument central to Massive
MIMO: fluctuations in individual channel coefficients average out, and
coarse decisions work well.

REMARK 2 (paper). The exponential-fit behavior is only accurate for large
M. Hence the heuristic methods (III-C, III-D) become more accurate as M
grows relative to K, which is precisely the Massive MIMO operating point.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  EQUAL VS. UNEQUAL POWER ALLOCATION IN MASSIVE MIMO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The question "should we transmit at full power?" has been asked repeatedly
in the Massive MIMO literature, with different answers depending on the
setting:

  • In SINGLE-CELL Massive MIMO with a centralized array and sum-rate
    objective, the optimal solution is typically "close to full power"
    (e.g., Ngo, Larsson, Marzetta 2013). The system is interference-
    limited only by MUI from the serving cell itself, and equal or
    slightly-tapered power suffices.

  • In MULTI-CELL Massive MIMO with inter-cell interference, optimal
    power is lower than full power, reflecting a "cell-edge help,
    cell-center back off" policy (Björnson et al. 2015–2017).

  • In CELL-FREE Massive MIMO with max-min criterion (our paper),
    the same "back off when close" intuition applies, but now the
    "cell" is the footprint of a single AP: an AP with a nearby user
    should back off, because otherwise it overrides the max-min. This
    is a cleaner and starker version of the multi-cell story.

The Nayebi et al. 2017 paper is the first to explicitly map out the
quantitative p_m* vs β_m^max relationship via the scatter plot (Figure 3)
and exploit it for heuristic design.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2  EXPONENTIAL FIT AS A DESIGN HEURISTIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Empirical curve-fitting of optimal solutions is an old technique in
optimization and control — sometimes called "lookup-table control" or
"function-approximation control". In the communications literature, it
appears in:

  • Bandwidth-allocation literature (Kelly et al., 1998): offline
    computation of Lagrange-dual price curves, then online use.

  • Reinforcement-learning-based power control (various 2018–2024
    deep-learning papers): train a neural net to mimic the optimal
    power allocation.

The exponential rule p_m = e^(−α β_m^max) is simpler than these ML
approaches and has the virtue of being EXPLAINABLE: one scalar parameter
α, one summary statistic β_m^max.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3  DECENTRALIZED POWER CONTROL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The idea of letting each AP make its own power decision without central
coordination has a long history in:

  • Wireless ad-hoc networks (Goodman & Mandayam, 2000): distributed
    game-theoretic power control.
  • Cellular networks with autonomous cells (3GPP Rel-8 LTE ICIC: inter-
    cell interference coordination via X2 signaling).
  • Massive MIMO scalable solutions (Björnson & Sanguinetti 2020: the
    "Scalable Cell-Free Massive MIMO" framework, directly motivated by
    the limitations of centralized approaches like Section III-A).

The heuristic uniform scheme (III-D) is the first cell-free massive MIMO
power control method with NO backhaul load for η_mk. Björnson &
Sanguinetti's later "scalable" paradigm generalizes this to other design
decisions (combining vectors, pilot assignment, etc.).

5.1  DEMONSTRATION OF p_m vs β_m^max SCATTER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Assume a small system: M = 8 APs, K = 2 users, ρ_f = 1 (normalized).
Suppose β_mk (normalized by noise variance) is:

       β (M×K) =
         ┌              ┐
         │  100    1    │  AP 1: very close to user 1
         │   50    2    │  AP 2
         │   10    5    │  AP 3
         │    5   10    │  AP 4
         │    2   50    │  AP 5
         │    1  100    │  AP 6
         │  0.5    1    │  AP 7: far from everyone
         │  0.3  0.3    │  AP 8: very far
         └              ┘

β_m^max per AP: [100, 50, 10, 10, 50, 100, 1, 0.3].

ASSUME (for illustration; we're not solving (6)) that the max-min
optimal power pattern looks like:

       p_m^opt  ≈  e^(−α · β_m^max),   with α = 0.03  (say).

Compute the implied p_m^opt:

   AP 1: e^(−0.03·100)  = e^(−3)    ≈ 0.050
   AP 2: e^(−0.03·50)   = e^(−1.5)  ≈ 0.223
   AP 3: e^(−0.03·10)   = e^(−0.3)  ≈ 0.741
   AP 4: e^(−0.03·10)   = e^(−0.3)  ≈ 0.741
   AP 5: e^(−0.03·50)   = e^(−1.5)  ≈ 0.223
   AP 6: e^(−0.03·100)  = e^(−3)    ≈ 0.050
   AP 7: e^(−0.03·1)    = e^(−0.03) ≈ 0.970
   AP 8: e^(−0.03·0.3)  = e^(−0.009)≈ 0.991

Interpretation:
  • Close APs (1, 6): ~5% power — heavily throttled.
  • Medium APs (2, 5): ~22% power.
  • Moderate APs (3, 4): ~74% power.
  • Far APs (7, 8): ~97–99% power — effectively full.

The sum of all power (if they were fighting for a global sum budget)
would be ~3.99, i.e., ~50% of the total 8·1 = 8 available. This matches
the paper's qualitative observation that "total transmit power for CB
with optimal allocation is much less than the total power limit".

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2  APPLYING THE UNIFORM COEFFICIENT RULE (10) TO THE SAME EXAMPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Assume γ_mk = β_mk (perfect estimation) for simplicity.

For AP 1: Σᵢ γ_{1,i} = 100 + 1 = 101.
          η_1 = p_1 / 101 = 0.050 / 101 ≈ 4.95 · 10⁻⁴.

For AP 2: Σᵢ γ_{2,i} = 50 + 2 = 52.
          η_2 = 0.223 / 52 ≈ 4.29 · 10⁻³.

For AP 3: Σᵢ γ_{3,i} = 10 + 5 = 15.
          η_3 = 0.741 / 15 ≈ 4.94 · 10⁻².

For AP 4: Σᵢ γ_{4,i} = 5 + 10 = 15.
          η_4 = 0.741 / 15 ≈ 4.94 · 10⁻².

For AP 5: Σᵢ γ_{5,i} = 2 + 50 = 52.
          η_5 = 0.223 / 52 ≈ 4.29 · 10⁻³.

For AP 6: Σᵢ γ_{6,i} = 1 + 100 = 101.
          η_6 = 0.050 / 101 ≈ 4.95 · 10⁻⁴.

For AP 7: Σᵢ γ_{7,i} = 0.5 + 1 = 1.5.
          η_7 = 0.970 / 1.5 ≈ 0.647.

For AP 8: Σᵢ γ_{8,i} = 0.3 + 0.3 = 0.6.
          η_8 = 0.991 / 0.6 ≈ 1.65.

OBSERVATION. η_m is LARGEST at the APs that are FARTHEST from users
(APs 7, 8). This is because those APs have small γ_mi values, so even
a big η_m costs very little budget per user. Meanwhile, η_m is tiny at
APs 1 and 6 because their γ values are huge and even a small η consumes
the budget fast.

The contribution of each AP to user 1's received signal (numerator of
Eq. 5) is √(η_m) · γ_{m,1}. Let's compute:

   AP 1: √(4.95·10⁻⁴) · 100  ≈  0.0223 · 100  =  2.23
   AP 2: √(4.29·10⁻³) · 50   ≈  0.0655 · 50   =  3.28
   AP 3: √(4.94·10⁻²) · 10   ≈  0.222 · 10    =  2.22
   AP 4: √(4.94·10⁻²) · 5    ≈  0.222 · 5     =  1.11
   AP 5: √(4.29·10⁻³) · 2    ≈  0.0655 · 2    =  0.131
   AP 6: √(4.95·10⁻⁴) · 1    ≈  0.0223 · 1    =  0.0223
   AP 7: √(0.647)      · 0.5 ≈  0.804 · 0.5   =  0.402
   AP 8: √(1.65)       · 0.3 ≈  1.285 · 0.3   =  0.386

Sum: 2.23 + 3.28 + 2.22 + 1.11 + 0.131 + 0.022 + 0.402 + 0.386 ≈ 9.78.

Squared (× ρ_f = 1):   ≈ 95.7.
So SINR_1 numerator ≈ 95.7.

For the denominator (interference + noise):
   1 + ρ_f · Σₘ β_{m,1} · Σᵢ η_m · γ_{m,i}
 = 1 + Σₘ β_{m,1} · η_m · Σᵢ γ_{m,i}
 = 1 + Σₘ β_{m,1} · p_m                    (by definition of η_m in (10))
 = 1 + (100·0.050 + 50·0.223 + 10·0.741 + 5·0.741 + 2·0.223 + 1·0.050
       + 0.5·0.970 + 0.3·0.991)
 = 1 + (5.00 + 11.15 + 7.41 + 3.705 + 0.446 + 0.050 + 0.485 + 0.297)
 = 1 + 28.54
 = 29.54.

SINR_1 ≈ 95.7 / 29.54 ≈ 3.24.
R_1 = log₂(1 + 3.24) = log₂(4.24) ≈ 2.08 bits/s/Hz.

By symmetry, SINR_2 ≈ 3.24 and R_2 ≈ 2.08.

COMPARE: if we had used FULL POWER (ignoring the exponential fit),
   p_m = 1 for all m,
   Σᵢ γ_{m,i} is the same,
   η_m = 1 / Σᵢ γ_{m,i}.

The denominator 1 + Σₘ β_{m,1} · 1 = 1 + 168.8 ≈ 169.8 (worse!).

The numerator √(η_m) · γ_{m,1} contributions would be:
   AP 1: √(1/101) · 100    ≈ 9.95
   AP 2: √(1/52)  · 50     ≈ 6.93
   AP 3: √(1/15)  · 10     ≈ 2.58
   AP 4: √(1/15)  · 5      ≈ 1.29
   AP 5: √(1/52)  · 2      ≈ 0.277
   AP 6: √(1/101) · 1      ≈ 0.0995
   AP 7: √(1/1.5) · 0.5    ≈ 0.408
   AP 8: √(1/0.6) · 0.3    ≈ 0.387
   Sum ≈ 21.92
   Squared ≈ 480.5.

Full-power SINR_1 = 480.5 / 169.8 ≈ 2.83. R_1 ≈ log₂(3.83) ≈ 1.94 bits/s/Hz.

And compare to the heuristic uniform scheme (III-D) at R_1 ≈ 2.08. The
heuristic wins by ~7% in rate, despite being much simpler computationally.

OF COURSE, this is a stylized example with a hand-picked α. In real
deployments the gain of III-D vs. III-B is 5–20% in 5%-outage rate
depending on (M, K, ρ_f, geometry), and the win of III-A (true optimum)
over III-D is a similar few percent.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.3  PRACTICE PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM. In the heuristic uniform scheme (III-D) with M = 100, K = 10,
assume γ_mk = β_mk and that the β_mk have been pre-computed. Using
α = 5 × 10⁹ (chosen to match a typical urban scenario with β ~ 10⁻¹⁰),

(a) If AP m has β_{m, k=1} = 5·10⁻¹⁰ and β_{m, k} = 10⁻¹¹ for k=2,…,10
    (very close to user 1, far from others), compute p_m and η_m.

(b) If AP m has β_{m, k} = 10⁻¹¹ for all k = 1,…,10 (a "bystander" AP),
    compute p_m and η_m.

(c) Which AP has larger η_m? Interpret.

SOLUTION.

(a) β_m^max = max_k β_mk = 5·10⁻¹⁰.
    p_m = exp(−α · β_m^max) = exp(−5·10⁹ · 5·10⁻¹⁰) = exp(−2.5) ≈ 0.082.
    Σᵢ γ_{m,i} = 5·10⁻¹⁰ + 9·10⁻¹¹ = 5.9·10⁻¹⁰.
    η_m = 0.082 / (5.9·10⁻¹⁰) ≈ 1.39 · 10⁸.

(b) β_m^max = 10⁻¹¹.
    p_m = exp(−α · β_m^max) = exp(−5·10⁹ · 10⁻¹¹) = exp(−0.05) ≈ 0.951.
    Σᵢ γ_{m,i} = 10 · 10⁻¹¹ = 10⁻¹⁰.
    η_m = 0.951 / 10⁻¹⁰ = 9.51 · 10⁹.

(c) The bystander AP (b) has η_m ≈ 9.51·10⁹, nearly 70× larger than the
    close AP (a)'s η_m ≈ 1.39·10⁸.
    INTERPRETATION: the uniform coefficient η_m is inversely related to
    Σᵢ γ_mi, not directly to the "importance" of the AP. A bystander AP
    has tiny γ_mi's, so a given η_m costs only a small fraction of its
    budget per user. The AP can "afford" a big η_m. Of course, the
    actual contribution to the received signal, √(η_m) · γ_mk, is still
    small because γ_mk is small. But the budget-normalized coefficient
    η_m is large.

This illustrates a subtle point: η_m itself is an allocation variable,
not an absolute power. The actual per-user power spent is η_m · γ_mi,
which is small for the bystander.

PART 6 — SECTION SUMMARY

WHAT WE COVERED IN THIS FILE:
  • III-B: Full Power Transmission. Convex (Proposition 1, proof by
    AM-GM), but gives poor performance. Motivates the need for
    non-full-power solutions.
  • III-C: Heuristic Fixed-Power Transmission. Per-AP budget is set by
    p_m = e^(−α β_m^max), with α fitted offline. Same online complexity
    as III-B but ~140% improvement in 5%-outage rate.
  • III-D: Heuristic Uniform Coefficients. η_mk = η_m for all k, with
    η_m = p_m / Σᵢ γ_mi. Closed form — no optimization needed. Fully
    decentralized (no backhaul for η).
  • Counterintuitive observation: at the max-min optimum for CB, most APs
    transmit at LESS than full power. Mechanism: nearby APs generate
    disproportionate interference and must back off.
  • Complexity/performance hierarchy: Optimal > III-C ≈ III-D ≫ Full Power.

KEY TAKEAWAYS:
  1. The full-power baseline is a USEFUL SANITY CHECK, not a practical
     strategy. It proves that max-min optima are INTERIOR points.
  2. The exponential rule p_m = e^(−α β_m^max) captures the empirical
     structure of optimal power allocation in massive cell-free systems.
  3. The uniform coefficient assumption η_mk = η_m ∀k works because in
     the M ≫ K regime, HOW AP m divides its budget among users matters
     less than TOTAL power spent.
  4. III-D is the first cell-free massive MIMO power control with ZERO
     backhaul load — foundational for later "scalable" frameworks.

EQUATIONS INTRODUCED IN THIS FILE:
  (8)  Max-min with per-AP equality constraint at full power.
  (9)  Max-min with per-AP equality at heuristic p_m.
  (10) Uniform coefficient η_m = p_m / Σᵢ γ_mi (closed form).
  (Proposition 1) Concavity of SINR_k(η), proof via AM-GM.
